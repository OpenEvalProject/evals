## Abstract

Cerebral blood flow is highly sensitive to changes in CO 2 /H + where an increase in CO 2 /H + causes vasodilation and increased blood flow. Tissue CO 2 /H + also functions as the main stimulus for breathing by activating chemosensitive neurons that control respiratory output. Considering that CO 2 /H + -induced vasodilation would accelerate removal of CO 2 /H + and potentially counteract the drive to breathe, we hypothesize that chemosensitive brain regions have adapted a means of preventing vascular CO 2 /H + -reactivity. Here, we show in rat that purinergic signaling, possibly through P2Y 2/4 receptors, in the retrotrapezoid nucleus (RTN) maintains arteriole tone during high CO 2 /H + and disruption of this mechanism decreases the CO2ventilatory response. Our discovery that CO 2 /H + -dependent regulation of vascular tone in the RTN is the opposite to the rest of the cerebral vascular tree is novel and fundamentally important for understanding how regulation of vascular tone is tailored to support neural function and behavior, in this case the drive to breathe.

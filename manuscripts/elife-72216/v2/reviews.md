Acceptance summary:

This manuscript provides a thorough interrogation of how various modifications to ssDNA and dsDNA repair templates affect genome editing efficiency in three commonly used animals models (Caenorhabditis elegans, zebrafish, mice) and human cell lines. The authors convincingly demonstrate that 5' TEG modifications dramatically boost knock-in efficiency, prevent concatemer formation, and reduce imprecise editing. In zebrafish and human cells an additional methylated RNA modification (2'OMe-RNA::TEG) at the 5' end of the donor resulted in improved editing efficiency compared to TEG alone. The authors also demonstrate that a free 3'OH in donors is necessary for efficient genome editing, providing insight into the repair mechanism. This information will enable researchers to increase the efficiency of gene editing with homology templates.

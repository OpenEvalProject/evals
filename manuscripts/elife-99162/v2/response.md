1) According to the Reviewers, a deeper investigation of the relationship between fat accumulation and inflammation is missing. Thanks for your good suggestions. We have addressed additional data in the revised manuscript to determine the relationship between fat accumulation and inflammation.

In our study, we have determined that overexpression Mir802 activates the canonical and canonical NF-κB pathways, which can increase CCL2 secretion leading to recruit macrophage. Then, we clarified that Mir802 indirectly stimulates lipogenic gene sterol regulatory element-binding protein 1 a (SREBP1a) expression via western blot (Figure 7B-D), Chip-qPCR (Figure 7F-G), DNA pull-down assays (Figure 7 H-I) and luciferase assays (Figure 7J).

As previously described, Srebp1a is a well-established regulator of lipid synthesis and required for macrophage phagocytosis lipid synthesis (doi: 10.1073/pnas.1813458115, doi: 10.1016/j.tcb.2020.09.006). Altogether, these data show that Mir802 indirectly regulates lipid droplet formation through SREBP1 and ultimately promotes macrophage to engulf more lipid droplets, promoting the polarization of macrophages.

Taken together, these data support macrophages as an important mediator for adipose Mir802–induced systemic inflammation in obesity. We have added these data in the manuscript, these data could demonstrate that Mir802 increased CCL2 level, which is the mediates to regulate the communication between adipocytes and macrophages.

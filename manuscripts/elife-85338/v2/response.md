– Given the asymmetric nature of the funnel plots, an Egger's test could also be run for additional assessment of publication bias in addition to the trim and fill.

We conducted Egger’s test for funnel plot asymmetry. The results were significant for depression, anxiety, and eating disorders, implying potential publication bias arising from "small study" effects. Nonetheless, other factors, such as heterogeneity of study populations, covariates, or outcome definitions, could also account for the funnel plot asymmetry. The "trim and fill" method suggested that the results for anxiety and depression were robust to this type of publication bias. In contrast, results for eating disorders were somewhat less robust (Line 335; Supplemental Table 9). Please note that the power of this method to detect bias will be low with small numbers of studies. We have added this data to the Results and Discussion sections of our manuscript.

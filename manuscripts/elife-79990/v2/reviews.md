## Review text

DOI: [10.7554/eLife.79990.sa0](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.79990.sa0)

Langerhans cells, found in the epidermis and mucosal epithelium, capture pathogens via a C-type lectin called langerin. The capture leads to the formation of a membrane vesicle called a Birbeck granule. Here the authors use cryo-electron tomography to show, for the first time, how langerin forms a zippered lattice. They use their structure to design mutations to disrupt lattice formation and show that this interferes with HIV internalization.

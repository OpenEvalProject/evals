## Response text

DOI: [10.7554/eLife.03587.036](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.03587.036)

The revised manuscript features the following editorial and experimental amendments.

1) We streamlined the text, with a shortened Introduction and Discussion as recommended.

2) We included cytological experiments providing compelling evidence that Caulobacter S-phase cells are encased in a polysaccharidic capsule.

2a) First, negative staining fluorescence microscopy revealed a thick layer that excludes FITC-labeled dextran from the delta-hvyA (capsulated) mutant versus the delta-CCNA00163 (non-capsulated) mutant in which a component of the export machinery has been inactivated. These fluorescence images and quantification are show in the new Figure 4.

2b) Second, atomic force microscopy of the same strains revealed a smoother surface on delta-CCNA00163 cells compared to delta-hvyA cells (new Figure 5). The difference in surface roughness is consistent with the biophysical properties expected for an amorphous layer of polysaccharides.

2c) Third, we used live cell fluorescence microscopy of cells expressing a different component of the capsule export machinery (CCNA00162, CCNA00163 and CCNA00168) as full-length translational fusion to mCherry to find that the mCherry-derived fluorescence emanates through the cell, suggesting that the export machinery (and thus the exported capsule) is not localized to specific subcellular site. These images are shown in Figure 4–figure supplement 1).

2d) Lastly, negative stain fluorescence microscopy of Caulobacter (NA1000) WT cells using FITC-dextran revealed that S-phase (stalked and/or constricted) cells exclude the dye. Cells that do not exclude the dye are stalkless and non-constricted (and thus in G1-phase). This important result that reinforces our conclusion that G1-cells are non-capsulated is also shown in the new Figure 4.

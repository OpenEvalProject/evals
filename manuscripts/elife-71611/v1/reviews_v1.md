Acceptance summary:

The authors show that translation of the bacterial replication initiation protein, DnaA, is regulated in response to nutrient availability in Caulobacter crescentus. Regulation requires a short amino acid sequence near the N-terminus of DnaA, suggesting that the nascent protein chain can stall ribosomes in a condition-specific manner. This novel regulatory mechanism also occurs in Escherichia coli, and is likely to be a widespread strategy for modulating protein production in response to changes in growth conditions.

Reviewer 2 is correct in stating that the association between BMD and fracture is stronger for Tsimane women than men. Men’s BMD is not significantly associated with fracture, defined as grade ≥1 on the Genant scale (i.e. ~20–25% reduction in vertebral height, and a 10–20% reduction in area). However, men’s BMD is inversely associated with borderline vertebral deformity, defined as grade 0.5 on the Genant scale (i.e. <20% reduction in height; for details see Appendix 1—table 17 here: https://elifesciences.org/articles/48607). For all of these reasons, we do not think that results for men invalidate the use of fracture status to ascertain degree of bone fragility.
